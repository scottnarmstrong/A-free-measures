# Errata and deviations

This list separates printed errors from choices about scope, conventions and interpretation. De Philippis–Rindler (DPR) uses the numbering of arXiv:1601.06543v2, where theorems, lemmas, corollaries and remarks share one counter within each section; a display without a number is cited by its position in the proof. Alberti–Marchese (AM) uses arXiv:1408.2379v2, where theorems, lemmas, corollaries, propositions, remarks and numbered paragraphs share one counter within each section, so Lemma 4.8 is the eighth item of Section 4. Ambrosio–Kirchheim (AK) uses the numbering of the MPI-MIS preprint 21/2000 (Definitions, equations and theorems as printed there). A grouped entry may cover repeated occurrences of the same error. Counts refer to entries, including editorial misprints, rather than to occurrences. Proof supplements are listed as deviations when they fill an omitted argument without establishing that the printed claim is false.

## De Philippis–Rindler

### Errata

1. **Theorem 1.15, last sentence.** The paper concludes $\mathbf M^{\mathrm{met}}_d(\mathbb R^d)=\mathbf F_d(\mathbb R^d)$. Flat chains are compactly supported by definition, whereas metric currents of finite mass in $\mathbb R^d$ need not be (for $u\in L^1$ without compact support, the current $[\![u]\!]$ of AK Example 3.2 is one), and AK (Section 11) restrict their comparison to compactly supported currents for this reason ("flat FF-currents are compactly supported by definition"). The library proves the identification for compactly supported metric currents: each such $T$ induces a flat chain $S$ with $\int f\,dS=T(f,x_1,\dots,x_d)$, every flat chain arises in this way, and $T$ is determined by these values (`AFree.isTopFlatChain_iff_isMetricCurrent`). The first assertion, $\|T\|\ll\mathcal L^d$, holds for every metric current and is stated in that generality (`AFree.absolutelyContinuous_of_isAKMass`); only the set equality is restricted.

2. **Theorem 1.1, proof for general operators, decomposition of $R_j$.** The text prints $R_j=R^1_j+R^2_j+R^2_j$, while $R^1_j$, $R^2_j$, $R^3_j$ are the three terms defined next. Read $R_j=R^1_j+R^2_j+R^3_j$. No effect on the stated theorems.

3. **Lemma 2.1, proof, first display.** The Bessel kernel is written $b(s,d)=\mathcal F^{-1}[(1+4\pi|\xi|^2)^{-s/2}]$, while the operator is defined with $(1+4\pi^2|\xi|^2)^{-s/2}$ and the Fourier convention of Section 2.1 forces $4\pi^2$. Read $4\pi^2$. No effect on the stated theorems; the formalized statement uses $4\pi^2$ (`AFree.exists_compact_besselPotential`).

4. **Lemma 2.2, hypothesis (c).** The equi-integrability condition is printed $\lim_{\mathcal L^d(E)\to0}\sup_j\int_{B_1}f_j^-\,dx=0$, in which $E$ does not occur. Read $\sup_j\int_Ef_j^-\,dx$; this is what the Vitali argument in the proof uses. No effect on the stated theorems.

5. **Theorem 1.6, proof, the operator $\mathcal A$.** The index ranges are printed $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n=1,\dots,d$ and $h=1,\dots,r$, but $\mu^k_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_h\,i\,\alpha_{h+2}\cdots\alpha_r}$ has $r+1$ indices when $h=r$ and the first index is never replaced. Read $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ and $h=0,\dots,r-1$. No effect on the stated theorems.

6. **Theorem 1.7, proof, display for $\mathbb A(\xi)M$.** The constant is printed $-(4\pi)^{-2}$. Since $\mathbb A(\xi)$ carries the factor $(2\pi i)^2=-4\pi^2$, the identity holds with $-(2\pi)^{-2}$. The kernels computed from it are unchanged, so there is no effect on the stated theorems.

7. **Lemma 3.1 (lead-in, (i) and proof) and proof of Theorem 1.15, citations of AM.** The text cites [AM, Corollary 6.5] and [AM, Section 2.6]. DPR cite the journal version of AM ("to appear"); in arXiv v2 of AM the corollary is Corollary 6.4, item 6.5 is a remark, and the decomposability bundle is defined in Section 2.7 (Section 2.6 is a remark on the integration of measures). No effect on the stated theorems.

### Deviations

1. **Sections 1–3, scope.** The library formalizes Theorem 1.1 (every order, with Remarks 1.3 and 1.4), Theorems 1.5–1.7 and 1.10, Corollaries 1.8, 1.9, 1.12 and 1.13, Remark 1.11, Theorems 1.14 and 1.15, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 3.1. Remark 1.2, the closing sentence of Remark 1.4 on pseudo-differential equations and the literature discussion are not formalized, and Lemma 2.2 enters only as an auxiliary step. The paper deduces Theorem 1.15 from Schioppa's theorem on Alberti representations of metric currents (arXiv:1403.7768); Schioppa's paper is not used by the library, see Deviation 21.

2. **Section 3 (before the proof of Theorem 1.10), Corollary 1.9, Theorem 1.10, Remark 1.11, Corollary 1.12, norms on $k$-vectors.** The paper endows $\Lambda_k(\mathbb R^d)$ with the mass norm. The library writes $k$-vectors in coordinates with the Euclidean norm and takes $\|T\|$ and $\vec T$ with respect to it. The two polars differ by a positive scalar field bounded above and below, so the properties concerned (simplicity, vanishing of the interior product with a covector, span, containment in $\Lambda_k(V)$, and $\mu\ll\|T_i\|$) are unchanged. No effect on the stated theorems.

3. **Section 1 (1.1) and Section 2.1, measures, $\mathcal A$-free and the wave cone.** An $\mathbb R^m$-valued measure is a vector measure with values in $\mathbb R^\iota$ for a finite index type, and a measure on $\Omega$ is a finite measure on $\mathbb R^d$ whose total variation vanishes outside $\Omega$. The total variation is taken for the Euclidean norm, the polar and the singular part are computed coordinatewise, and $\mathcal A\mu=0$ is tested coordinatewise against real functions in $C^\infty_c(\Omega)$. The wave cone is the real kernel $\bigcup_{|\xi|=1}\ker\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\xi^\alpha A_\alpha$; the paper's symbol $(2\pi i)^k\sum_{|\alpha|=k}A_\alpha\xi^\alpha$ differs by a nonzero scalar, and equality of the two cones is proved (`AFree.mem_waveCone_iff_complexSymbol`). No effect on the stated theorems.

4. **Theorems 1.5–1.7 and Corollary 1.8, encoding of $\mathrm{BV}$, $\mathrm{BD}$ and higher derivatives.** The hypothesis $u\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathbb R^\ell)$ is encoded as $u\in L^1(\Omega)$ together with a finite measure $Du$ on $\Omega$ satisfying $\int_\Omega u_k\partial_j\varphi=-\int\varphi\,dDu_{kj}$; likewise $D^ru$ and $Eu$ are recorded on all index tuples (their symmetry is automatic), and $\mathrm{div}\,u\in L^2(\Omega)$ in Corollary 1.8 is a function $g\in L^2(\Omega)$ representing $\mathrm{div}\,u$ in the sense of distributions. The conclusion concerns the polar of the Lebesgue singular part at almost every point for its total variation. No effect on the stated theorems.

5. **Section 3 (definitions of currents), Corollaries 1.9 and 1.12, Theorem 1.10, Remark 1.11, normal currents.** A $k$-current of finite mass is a finite measure with values in $\Lambda_k(\mathbb R^d)$ (coordinates indexed by increasing $k$-tuples); it is normal on $\Omega$ if there is a finite $\Lambda_{k-1}$-valued measure $S$ on $\Omega$ with $\int\varphi\,dS_J=\sum_{j\notin J}\pm\int\partial_j\varphi\,dT_{J\cup\{j\}}$, that is, the boundary is tested on forms $\varphi\,dx^J$. $\mathbf N^{\mathrm{loc}}_k(\mathbb R^d)$ is encoded in polar form: a locally finite measure $\tau$ and a Borel field $P$ with $|P|=1$ $\tau$-a.e. whose boundary is $P'\tau'$ with $\tau'$ locally finite and $P'$ locally $\tau'$-integrable. The measure $\mu\in\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$ of Theorem 1.10 vanishes outside $\Omega$ and is finite on compact subsets of $\Omega$, and the "Equivalently" statement of Theorem 1.10 is a separate theorem (`AFree.interiorProduct_eq_zero_iff_mem_kvecSpan`). No effect on the stated theorems.

6. **Remark 1.3, order and "easy to check".** The identity $\Lambda_{\tilde{\mathcal A}}=\Lambda_{\mathcal A}\times\mathbb R^n$ is printed for $k\ge1$, and the library assumes $1\le k$ in the whole statement (`AFree.polar_mem_waveCone_of_eq_measure`); this is the reading of $k\in\mathbb N$ forced by multi-indices ranging over $(\mathbb N\cup\{0\})^d$. For $k=0$ the extension of Theorem 1.1 to $\mathcal A\mu=\sigma$ is false: with $\mathcal A=\mathrm{Id}$ and $\sigma=\mu$ singular one has $\Lambda_{\mathcal A}=\{0\}$. The "easy to check" assertions (the wave cone of $\tilde{\mathcal A}$, proportionality of the polars, and the passage between the singular sets of $|\mu|$ and $|\tilde\mu|$) are proved. No effect on the stated theorems.

7. **Remark 1.4, "suitable regularity".** The hypothesis is formalized as: the entries of $A_\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le k$ are $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$, and $\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}A_\alpha(x)\partial^\alpha\mu=0$ is tested coordinatewise against $\varphi e_j$ with $\varphi\in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$ (`AFree.polar_mem_waveCone_var`). "Using essentially the same proof" is replaced by a complete argument in which the coefficients are evaluated at $x_0$ after the blow-up, the commutators with the coefficients being derivatives of finite measures of mass $O(r_j)$. No effect on the stated theorems.

8. **Theorem 1.1, proof for first-order operators, from (2.9) to (2.12).** The paper takes Fourier transforms, multiplies by $[\mathbb A(\xi)P_0]^*$ and divides by $1+|\mathbb A(\xi)P_0|^2$ to obtain $T_0,T_1,T_2$ (Hörmander–Mihlin, Lemma 2.1). The library applies the transposed matrix to the equation and inverts the resulting constant-coefficient divergence-form operator exactly by its Newtonian potential in physical space, using a weak-type $(1,1)$ bound for the gradient of that potential (from a companion library on elliptic regularity) and the local integrability of its kernel. The term $T_1$ does not occur, since the functions involved have compact support. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.polar_mem_waveConeFirstOrder`).

9. **Theorem 1.1, proof for general operators, from (2.14) on.** The paper writes the cut-off remainder with variable coefficients $b_\alpha(x)$ and treats the terms of $\widetilde R_j$ by pseudo-differential operators of order $0$ (Stein, Chapter VI). The library commutes the cut-off with the top-order part by a reverse Leibniz identity, so that all terms are constant-coefficient derivatives of smooth compactly supported functions; after the Fourier transform the multipliers lie in explicit symbol classes, the order-zero one is handled by a weak-type $(1,1)$ Hörmander–Mihlin estimate and the others as convolutions with $L^1$ kernels, which are compact. Lemma 2.1 is not used in this proof, and the physical-space inversion of Deviation 8 is not available for $k\ge2$. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.polar_mem_waveCone`).

10. **Theorem 1.1, proof, choice of $x_0$ and $r_j$, items (i)–(iii), and (2.2), (2.3), (2.5).** The paper obtains a nonzero tangent measure $\nu$ of $|\mu|^s$ at $x_0$ (Preiss; Rindler), then (2.2) and (2.3), and chooses $\varepsilon_j$ in (2.5) by lower semicontinuity of the total variation. The library chooses the scales by a Besicovitch differentiation (doubling) argument, so that the blow-ups give mass at least $t^{d+1}$ to a fixed small cube; $\varepsilon_j$ is chosen from $\nu_j$ alone by inner regularity, no limit $\nu$ is formed, and the contradiction is obtained directly from concentration of $\nu_j$ against $L^1$-precompactness of the mollified sequence. (2.2) and (2.3) are therefore not proved as such. No effect on the stated theorems.

11. **Theorem 1.1, proof, items (iii) and (iii').** The coercivity bound $|\mathbb A^k(\xi)P_0|\ge c|\xi|^k$, the choice of $x_0$ in $E$, and the measurability of $E$ (needed to speak of $|\mu|^s(E)\gt0$) are asserted without proof. The library proves the bound by compactness of the unit sphere, uses $|\mathbb A^k(\xi)P_0|=(2\pi)^k\bigl|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\xi^\alpha A_\alpha P_0\bigr|$ to pass to the real symbol, and proves measurability also for variable coefficients. No effect on the stated theorems.

12. **Lemma 2.1, statement and proof.** The paper proves $L^1\to L^1$ compactness from $b=b_{1,\varepsilon}+b_{2,\varepsilon}$ and concludes for $L^p$ "by Hölder's inequality", which is the inequality $\|f\|_p\le\|f\|_1^\theta\|f\|_q^{1-\theta}$ combined with $L^1\to L^q$ boundedness for $q\lt p(d,s)$. The library approximates $b(s,d)$ directly in $L^p$ by compactly supported continuous kernels, so Young's inequality gives convergence in operator norm $L^1\to L^p$ and compactness follows from Arzelà–Ascoli, with no interpolation. The statement is formalized as the existence of a compact operator $L^1_c(B_1)\to L^p$ agreeing with the Bessel potential on smooth $u$ supported in the open unit ball, for $1\le p\lt p(d,s)$; the paper defines the operator on Schwartz functions and asserts an extension to $L^1_c(B_1)$. No effect on the stated theorems.

13. **Theorems 1.5 and 1.6, proofs, wave cones.** The wave cones of the curl operator and of the operator of Theorem 1.6 are quoted from Fonseca–Müller (Remark 3.5(iii), Example 3.10(d)). The library computes them, namely $\Lambda_{\mathcal A}=\{a\otimes\xi:\ \xi\neq0\}$ and $\Lambda_{\mathcal A}=\{a\otimes\xi^{\otimes r}:\ \xi\neq0\}$, including that $\mathcal A\mu=0$ holds exactly for $r$-th derivatives. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.polar_lebesgueSingularPart_eq_tensor`, `AFree.polar_lebesgueSingularPart_eq_tensorPow`).

14. **Theorem 1.7, proof, the operator.** The paper uses the contracted operator $(\mathcal A\mu)_{jk}=\sum_i\partial_{ik}\mu^j_i+\partial_{ij}\mu^k_i-\partial_{jk}\mu^i_i-\partial_{ii}\mu^k_j$ on symmetric matrices and takes its kernel from Fonseca–Müller (Example 3.10(e)). The library uses the uncontracted Saint-Venant operator $(\mathcal AM)_{ijkl}=\partial_{jl}M_{ik}+\partial_{ik}M_{jl}-\partial_{il}M_{jk}-\partial_{jk}M_{il}$ on all of $\mathbb R^{d\times d}$, applied to $Eu$ (symmetric), and computes its kernel on symmetric matrices in full, namely $\{a\otimes\xi+\xi\otimes a\}$. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.polar_lebesgueSingularPart_eq_symmTensor`).

15. **Corollary 1.8, proof, "we must have $a(x)\cdot b(x)=0$".** The library shows that the singular part of the measure $\mathrm{div}\,u$ vanishes when $\mathrm{div}\,u$ is a function, by uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition applied to $\mathrm{tr}\,Eu=\mathrm{div}\,u$, and then reads off $a\cdot b=0$ from $\mathrm{tr}(a\odot b)=a\cdot b$. Only $\mathrm{div}\,u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is used. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.polar_lebesgueSingularPart_eq_symmTensor_orth`).

16. **Corollary 1.9, derivation.** The paper deduces it from Theorem 1.5 by identifying a boundaryless normal $(d-1)$-current with $\star Du$, $u\in\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)$. This needs a Poincaré lemma for normal currents, a localization from $\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)$ to $\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)$ and the Hodge star, none of which is carried out. The library obtains Corollary 1.9 as the case $\Omega=\mathbb R^d$, $k_i=d-1$, $\partial T_i=0$ of Theorem 1.10, with $\dim\ker\omega=d-1$ for $\omega\neq0$. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.exists_hyperplane_of_boundaryless`).

17. **Theorem 1.10, proof, (3.3), "clearly $\mu^s\ll|\boldsymbol T|^s$", (3.4) and "a simple exercise in linear algebra".** The symbol identity (3.3), the absolute continuity of $\mu^s$ with respect to $|\boldsymbol T|^s$, the factorization (3.4) of the polar of $T_i$ through the polar of $\boldsymbol T$, and the equivalence of the two formulations are all proved. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.exists_covector_interiorProduct_eq_zero`).

18. **Remark 1.11.** The claim that every $w\in\Lambda_{d-2}(V)$ is simple when $\dim V=d-1$, the application of Theorem 1.10 to currents in $\mathbf N^{\mathrm{loc}}_{d-2}$ (which requires localization to bounded open sets), and the normality and non-simplicity of the example in $\mathbb R^5$ are proved. In the Euclidean normalization the example has $\vec T=(e_1\wedge e_2+e_3\wedge e_4)/\sqrt2$ and $\|T\|=\sqrt2\,\mathcal H^4\llcorner\{x_5=0\}$. The statement writes $\mathcal H^4$ as Mathlib's Hausdorff measure for the sup metric on $\mathbb R^5$, which on the coordinate hyperplane $\{x_5=0\}$ equals $\mathcal L^4$, the Euclidean $\mathcal H^4$. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.ae_isSimpleKVec_of_isLocNormalCurrent`, `AFree.not_ae_isSimpleKVec_example`).

19. **Theorem 1.14, proof.** The paper quotes [AM, Theorem 1.1] to get $V(\mu,x)=\mathbb R^d$, then Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 1.12. The library uses only the direction of AM Section 4 stating that $V\neq\mathbb R^d$ on a set of positive measure produces a Lipschitz function that is not differentiable there (Lemma 4.8, Proposition 4.10, Lemma 4.11, Proposition 7.4, Lemma 7.3), with an explicit iteration of the single-step Lemma 4.8 and a Dini-type robustness argument in place of Baire category (Lemma 4.7, Propositions 4.2–4.3, Theorem 4.4 and Lemma 4.13 are not used). That $V=\mathbb R^d$ almost everywhere implies $\mu\ll\mathcal L^d$ is proved by a selection of countably many rational cones, the curve families of the proof of Lemma 7.3 and Corollary 1.12 applied to the associated one-dimensional normal currents, instead of AM Sections 5–6; AM Theorem 1.1(i) is not used, and AM Theorem 1.1 is for finite measures, so the locally finite case is reduced by restriction to balls. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.absolutelyContinuous_of_ae_differentiableAt`).

20. **Lemma 3.1, proof.** For (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i) the library uses Corollary 1.12 to get $\mu\ll\mathcal L^d$ and then $V(\mu,x)=\mathbb R^d$ from the case $\mu\ll\mathcal L^n$ of [AM, Proposition 2.9(iii)], instead of the corresponding implication of [AM, Corollary 6.4]. That corollary is stated for $n\ge2$ and fails for $n=1$ (AM Remark 6.5(iv)), so the paper's proof does not cover $d=1$, where both sides reduce to $\mu\ll\mathcal L^1$ and the lemma is true; the formalized Lemma 3.1 holds for every $d$. For (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii) it proves $\mu\ll\mathcal L^d$ as in Deviation 19 and takes $T_i=e_i\,e^{-|x|^2}\mathcal L^d$. No effect on the stated theorems (`AFree.decompBundle_eq_top_iff`; the existence of $V$ is `AFree.exists_isDecompBundle`).

21. **Theorem 1.15, proof, "by the very definition of decomposability bundle".** Schioppa's theorem gives Alberti representations of $\|T\|$ in the direction of 1-Lipschitz maps $\pi^j$, that is, curves $\gamma$ with $(\pi^j\circ\gamma)'$ in a cone, not curves with tangent in a cone; deducing $V(\|T\|,x)=\mathbb R^d$ requires differentiability of $\pi^j$ along $V$, which is [AM, Theorem 1.1(i)] and is not cited. The library avoids Schioppa and the decomposability bundle: it writes $T(f,\pi)=\int f\det D\pi\,d\nu$ with $\nu=T(\cdot,x_1,\dots,x_d)$ (AK Theorem 3.5), so $\|T\|=|\nu|$; shows $|\nu|$ vanishes on cone-null compact sets using AM Proposition 7.4 and the continuity axiom; dominates $|\nu|$ by averages of curve measures (Rainwater's lemma, AM Corollary 7.2, with an exhaustion); and concludes with Corollary 1.12 applied to $d$ one-dimensional normal currents with polars in independent cones. The flat-chain statement of Erratum 1 is proved by the explicit correspondence $u\mapsto u_\#$ between $L^1$ densities and metric currents, with $\mathbf F_d(\mathbb R^d)$ encoded as the compactly supported finite measures that are mass limits of compactly supported normal currents; the representation $T=u_\#$ for $\|T\|\ll\mathcal L^d$ (AK Theorem 3.8, one direction) is proved from $\nu$, and AK Theorems 3.7, 3.8 and 11.1 are not quoted. No effect on the stated theorems.

## Alberti–Marchese

### Errata

1. **Lemma 4.8, proof, Step 2.** The sets $E'_k$ are defined "for every integer $k$ with $1\lt k\le d$", but the covering argument that follows needs all $k$ with $1\le k\le d$. Read $1\le k\le d$. No effect on the stated theorems.

2. **Lemma 4.8, proof, Step 5.** The point $x+hv$ is taken on $\partial B(\bar x,r')$ and $g(x+hv)=0$ is asserted because $\mathrm{supp}\,g\subset B(\bar x,r')$; the support is a closed subset of the closed ball, so $g$ need not vanish on the sphere. Choose $h$ so that $x+hv$ lies outside the ball (for example $h=4r$ after shrinking $r$); the other perturbations then enter, see Deviation 4. No effect on the stated theorems.

3. **Lemma 4.8, proof, Step 6.** The conclusion $|D_k(f'+\bar g)|\le1-\varepsilon'/2+o(1)\le1$ "for $r$ small enough" needs $o(1)\le\varepsilon'/2$, but $\varepsilon'\le r(r'-r)\to0$ with $r$, and the $o(1)$ terms (the variation of $e_k$ and of $D_kf'$ on the ball) need not be that small. Use instead a margin independent of $r$, namely $1-\|D_jf'\|_\infty\gt0$ from Step 1(b): the sign choice gives $|D_k(f'+\bar g)|\le1-s/2+o(1)$ with $s$ fixed, and the constraint $\varepsilon'\le r(r'-r)$ is needed only for the term $|g|/h$ in Step 5. No effect on the stated theorems.

4. **Lemma 4.11, statement (ii) and proof.** The statement gives $-\varepsilon\le D_eg\le1$ on $B'$. For $g=\varphi f$ one has $D_eg=\varphi D_ef+f\,D_e\varphi$, and the proof only bounds the second term by $\varepsilon$, which yields $-\varepsilon\le D_eg\le1+\varepsilon$. The proof also asks the cut-off to vanish "for $x\in B(\bar x,r')$"; it must vanish outside that ball. The upper bound $1+\varepsilon$ is absorbed by the fixed margin of the repaired Step 6 (Erratum 3), so there is no effect on the stated theorems.

5. **Lemma 7.3, proof, Step 3, footnote.** It is asserted that $G\cap B(k)$ is covered by finitely many $G_\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\mathcal G_k$, the paths contained in $B(k)$. The footnote only reparametrizes in $\mathbb R^n$ and does not keep the paths in the ball, and the claim fails when $k\tan\alpha$ is small. Write $x=(x\cdot e)e+y$ with $y\perp e$. A path $\gamma\in\mathcal G_k$ satisfies $\gamma(1)\cdot e-\gamma(0)\cdot e\ge\cos\alpha$ and $|\dot\gamma-(\dot\gamma\cdot e)e|\le\sin\alpha$, so if it meets $\{|y|\ge k-\delta\}$ it stays in $\{|y|\ge k-\delta-\sin\alpha\}$, where the extent of $B(k)$ in the direction $e$ is at most $2\sqrt{2k(\delta+\sin\alpha)}$. Hence, when $8k\tan\alpha\lt\cos\alpha$ and $\delta\lt\cos^2\alpha/(8k)-\sin\alpha$, no path of $\mathcal G_k$ meets $\{|y|\ge k-\delta\}$, while $B(k)\cap\{|y|\ge k-\delta\}$ contains segments in the direction $e$, which are $C$-curves of positive length. The proof is repaired by replacing $E_k$, in the second case, by $E_{k+1}\cap B(k)$, which is covered by finitely many $G_\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\mathcal G_{k+1}$. Lemma 7.3 itself is true. No effect on the stated theorems.

6. **Proposition 7.4, proof, Step 2, property (c).** The estimate is printed $|g(x+v)-g(v)|\le|v|/\tan\alpha$ for $v\in e^\perp$; read $g(x)$. Property (c') is deduced from (c) in this form. No effect on the stated theorems.

7. **Proposition 7.4, proof, Step 1.** "Contradicts the fact that $K$ is a $C$-curve" should read "$C$-null". No effect on the stated theorems.

### Deviations

1. **Sections 2.4 and 2.7, and Lemma 7.3(ii), tangents of rectifiable sets.** The approximate tangent line is encoded through the characterization of Section 2.4 (equality $\mathrm{Tan}(E,x)=\mathrm{Tan}(S,x)$ for $\mathcal H^1$-a.e. $x\in E\cap S$, for every $C^1$ curve $S$), so the test in the definition of the decomposability bundle is made against $C^1$ arcs, and Lemma 7.3(ii) is used in the form: for a.e. $t$, countably many $C^1$ arcs carry $\mathcal H^1$-almost all of $E_t$, with tangent in $C\cup(-C)$ at the point; the Lipschitz $C$-curves of the proof are replaced by countably many $C^1$ arcs by a Lusin-type approximation. The arcs must be fixed first, since the reading "each point lies on some $C^1$ arc with tangent in $C$" would be vacuous. $\mathcal H^1$ and Lipschitz constants are Euclidean, and Borel subspace fields are Borel maps into orthogonal projections. No effect on the stated theorems.

2. **Section 4.1, global frame.** Continuous orthonormal fields $e_1,\dots,e_n$ on all of $\mathbb R^n$, adapted to $V$ on $E$, are assumed; for a general compact $E$ there is a topological obstruction to extending a frame, and AM supply it in Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 4.4 by shrinking $E$, extending by Tietze and orthonormalizing. The library does the same inside the proof, with a frame obtained from Lusin's theorem applied to the field of orthogonal projections onto $V$ and Gram–Schmidt on the columns of $\mathrm{Id}-P$, instead of a measurable selection theorem. No effect on the stated theorems.

3. **Lemma 4.8, proof, Step 3 (and Step 6), continuity of $V$.** AM define the cone from $\alpha(\bar x,r)$, the oscillation of $V$ on $E\cap B(\bar x,r)$. The library uses the modulus of continuity of the frame, which is defined on all of $B(\bar x,2r)$ (needed in Step 6, where $e_j$ is evaluated off $E$) and only requires $e_k\perp V$ on $E$. No effect on the stated theorems.

4. **Lemma 4.8, proof, Steps 5 and 7, other perturbations.** After the choice of $h$ in Erratum 2, the point $x+hv$ lies outside $B'_i$ and may lie in another ball $B'_j$, where $f''=f'+\bar g_j$. The library fixes one $\varepsilon^*\le\min_ir_i^2$ after the finite family of balls is chosen and bounds the contribution of $\bar g_j(x+hv)$ by $\varepsilon^*/(2h)$. (For AM's own choice of $h$ on $\partial B'_i$ the statement that the estimate "involves only the restriction to $B'_i$" is correct.) No effect on the stated theorems.

5. **Lemma 7.3, proof, Step 3, measures on paths.** Corollary 7.2(ii) provides a probability measure $\sigma$ on the compact family $\mathcal F_k=\{\lambda_\gamma\}$, and Step 3 uses it as a measure on the space $\mathcal G_k$ of paths. This is legitimate but unstated: $\gamma\mapsto\lambda_\gamma$ is a continuous injection of the compact space $\mathcal G_k$ (the coordinate $\gamma\cdot e$ is strictly increasing along every path), hence a homeomorphism onto $\mathcal F_k$. The library applies Rainwater's lemma (as in Corollary 7.2) to the compact convex family $\{\int\lambda_\gamma\,d\tau(\gamma):\tau\in\mathcal P(\mathcal G_k)\}$, which gives $\tau$ on $\mathcal G_k$ directly. No effect on the stated theorems.

6. **Proposition 7.4, proof, Steps 1–3.** The properties (a)–(c) of $g$ "one readily checks", and the passage from (a), (b'), (c') to (i)–(iii) after mollification, are proved in full, without Rademacher's theorem. Step 1 is carried out with limsup sets and reverse Fatou instead of Hausdorff limits of compact sets and upper semicontinuity of $\mathcal L^1$. No effect on the stated theorems.

## Ambrosio–Kirchheim

### Errata

1. **Definition 2.6, paragraph after the definition.** The text says that if $\{\mu_i\}\subset\mathcal M(E)$ satisfy (2.3), then their infimum satisfies the same condition; the condition meant is (2.2), and (2.3) is the extended inequality, which is derived afterwards. Read (2.2). No effect on the stated theorems.

2. **Definition 2.6, extension (2.3).** It is stated that every $T\in\mathrm{MF}_k(E)$ of finite mass extends uniquely to $B^\infty(E)\times[\mathrm{Lip}(E)]^k$ by density of $\mathrm{Lip}_b(E)$ in $L^1(\|T\|)$. Subadditivity of $|T|$ controls $|T(f)|-|T(g)|$ but not $T(f)-T(g)$, so the density argument needs linearity in $f$: for $T(f)=\varepsilon(f)\int|f|\,d\mu$, with $\varepsilon(f)\in\{\pm1\}$ the sign of $f(x_0)$ at a point $x_0$ with $\mu(\{x_0\})=0$, $T$ is a $0$-dimensional metric functional of finite mass which for suitable $\varepsilon$ is not $L^1(\mu)$-continuous, and extensions satisfying (2.3) are not unique. For currents (Definition 3.1) the claim is true, since then $|T(f)-T(g)|=|T(f-g)|$, and this is all that is used afterwards; the library proves it for currents only. No effect on the stated theorems.

### Deviations

1. **Definitions 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1, encoding.** Metric functionals on $\mathbb R^d$ are real functions $T(f,\pi)$ of all pairs, only used on $\mathrm{Lip}_b(\mathbb R^d)\times\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb R^d)^k$; Lipschitz constants are Euclidean, and the mass $\|T\|$ is the least finite measure satisfying (2.2). The existence of this least measure is a separate theorem (`AFree.exists_isAKMass`). No effect on the stated theorems.

2. **Definition 2.6, "one can easily check".** That the infimum of admissible measures is admissible is proved: for two measures by splitting $f=f\varphi+f(1-\varphi)$ with Lipschitz $\varphi$ approximating a Hahn set, which uses only subadditivity in the first slot, and for arbitrary families by a decreasing sequence of admissible measures whose total masses converge to the infimum (a setwise decreasing limit of finite measures is a measure). No effect on the stated theorems.

3. **Example 3.2.** The continuity axiom for $[\![g]\!]$ is cited ("well known $w^*$-continuity properties of determinants in $W^{1,\infty}$") and $\|[\![g]\!]\|=|g|\mathcal L^k$ is "not hard to prove". The library proves the weak-$*$ continuity of all minors of Lipschitz maps under pointwise convergence with bounded Lipschitz constants, by induction on the size of the minor through the divergence form, and obtains $\|[\![g]\!]\|=|g|\mathcal L^k$ from the density $\nu=T(\cdot,x_1,\dots,x_k)$. No effect on the stated theorems.

4. **Theorem 3.5, proofs of (3.2), (3.3) and alternation.** AK outline (3.3) "first for affine functions, then for piecewise affine functions and then for Lipschitz and continuously differentiable functions" and prove (3.2) through triangulations and piecewise affine approximants converging in $W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The library proves the alternating property and the chain rule (3.2) for $E=\mathbb R^d$ by Taylor expansion, locality on open sets and Lipschitz partitions of unity, with no triangulation; the product rule (3.1) and the strong locality (iii) are not needed. No effect on the stated theorems.
